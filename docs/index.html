Why does it wave?

The hills and valleys are seasonal, not crises.

Electricity demand peaks in winter (heating, short days) and dips in spring/autumn (mild weather), so most countries' production rises and falls every year.

Solar follows the opposite cycle — high in summer, near-zero in December — which is why its band swells in the middle of each year.

To spot a real shock, look for changes in shape (e.g. a band that grows or shrinks vs. its usual rhythm), not the seasonal swings themselves.

The takeaway

When the world cracks, the lights flicker — but not always equally.

🇺🇦

Ukraine: a structural shock

The Russia–Ukraine war hit Europe's electricity at its heart — natural gas. Russian pipeline gas had set the marginal price of power across the continent for decades. When it stopped, day-ahead prices tripled, peaking above €700/MWh in summer 2022, and the energy mix structurally tilted: coal back on, then renewables and LNG to fill the gap.

🇮🇷

Iran: a softer ripple

The Iran flare-up has barely touched European electricity so far. Europe sits behind two buffers — full gas storage and a more diversified LNG supply — so a Middle-East shock now translates into a single-digit percent price wobble, not a triple-digit spike. The contrast is the lesson: the same shape of crisis can hit very differently depending on the grid that absorbs it.

⚓

The Strait of Hormuz — a slow-burn risk

Roughly 20% of global LNG and a quarter of seaborne oil pass through the Strait of Hormuz. The recent disruptions have already nudged LNG insurance premiums and freight rates higher, and our latest months of data show European prices drifting up again. It is not a 2022-style spike — yet — but a reminder that any choke point on the world's energy map sends ripples all the way to your electricity bill.

A conflict that erupts thousands of kilometres from your home rewrites the price tag on your bread, your heating, and the news on your screen. Energy is the most international good there is, and the electricity market — fast, transparent, hourly — is the cleanest mirror we have of those distant tremors.
